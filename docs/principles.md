# EGASE Principles

EGASE v0.1 is built around a small set of principles intended to preserve useful agent autonomy while making material software decisions governable.

1. **Reasoning before authority.** Agents may explore hypotheses and alternatives before those ideas become authoritative product decisions.
2. **Evidence before claim.** A claim must not exceed what its evidence actually demonstrates.
3. **Materiality before ceremony.** Governance effort should scale with risk, reversibility, scope, and consequence.
4. **Human authority where it matters.** Human review is required for genuinely material decisions, not for every reversible implementation detail.
5. **Open synthesis, bounded execution.** Hard constraints and approved intent define the problem; they should not unnecessarily prescribe the solution space.
6. **Runtime truth is distinct from source truth.** Source inspection, tests, deployment, runtime observation, and human usability are different evidence classes.
7. **Failure is evidence.** False starts, over-governance, and unsuccessful strategies should remain visible when they materially inform future decisions.
8. **Governance cost is itself a quality signal.** A control that repeatedly costs more than the risk it mitigates must justify its continued use.
9. **No evidence theater.** Extra fields, manifests, or gates are not valuable merely because they are machine-checkable.
10. **Tool and model neutrality.** The method should not depend on a single coding agent, model provider, cloud, framework, or delivery platform.
11. **Privacy by abstraction.** Public examples and case studies should preserve methodological learning without exposing identifiable client, sector, location, product, or proprietary-domain information.
